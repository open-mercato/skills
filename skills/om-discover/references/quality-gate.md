# Quality gate

Apply before showing the draft (step 6) and again before writing the file (step 7). Do not print the score unless the user asks for an audit. Score each item 0 (absent or contradicted), 1 (present but vague, assumed, or partial), 2 (concrete and supported).

## Evidence

1. Every claim carries a tag and a source pointer that exists.
2. No claim tagged above its support: no assumption dressed as an interview, no walkthrough dressed as data.
3. Every number has provenance on the same line.
4. Every benchmark has a link and a date checked.
5. Every quote appears verbatim in a cited note.
6. No persona, name, age, or situation appears that no source contains; every persona-derived line is under Hypotheses and tagged `[SYNTHETIC]`.
7. No section was written over an empty research folder without the user's explicit choice, and the coverage line says so when they chose it.

## Decisions and scope

8. Every decision, non-goal, and business rule has a stable id, a status, and a required path to change it, and either an owner (`active`) or the `proposal` status with a blocking open question naming who signs it.
9. Now completes one real job end to end; Later and Not doing are explicit.
10. Every open question is marked blocking or not and names who can answer it.
11. The Definition of Ready addendum for the mode is filled, or says what is missing.

## Readability

12. The brief is written for a reader who was not in the room: product, users, rules, and decisions named plainly; no method vocabulary except the evidence tags.
13. The coverage line is recomputed and matches the body.

## Critical gates

Do not show or write the brief with a zero in items 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, or 8. Fix the item, or move the section to the collection plan and say so.

## Slop signals to remove on sight

- "Users want", "customers need", "the market demands" with no source on the line.
- A persona introduced with a name and an age.
- A round number that appeared during the session and nowhere else.
- A competitor described from memory.
- A vision sentence that would fit any product in the category.
- A section whose length is unrelated to the amount of material behind it.
- A decision with no name next to it.
- A benchmark used as validation of our own idea.

## Final compression pass

1. What can be removed without weakening a decision?
2. Which sentence is abstract where a concrete claim with a source would be clearer?
3. Which assumption most needs to move to the assumption map with a test?
4. Does the brief help the next person act without inventing what is missing — or does it name what is missing?
